An overview for everyone working with the Islington Food Partnership

Building the food system Islington residents can rely on.

The Islington Food Partnership (IFP) already exists, already has funding secured to 2028, and is restructuring itself into four dedicated action groups so it can actually deliver against its plans, not just coordinate conversation about them. Alongside that, its shared food distribution hub with Camden is now recruiting the operator who will run it. This page is a single place to see the ambition behind both, how they connect to work already happening across London, and how it all stays coordinated.

Why this exists

Four action groups, one plan

Instead of one general coordinating group trying to cover everything, the partnership is organising around four specific ambitions — so people can commit to the part they actually care about, without having to sit through the rest.

Healthy and affordable food for all

Free, paid-for, and everything in between

Mapping the borough's food services so there's a genuine balance of free, paid-for and community-priced options, and connecting people to the right kind of support — not just calories, but nutrition, advice and dignity together.

Climate and nature emergency

Growing, waste and eating, as one system

Connecting food waste, composting, growing spaces and shared eating so they reinforce each other, rather than running as separate, disconnected programmes.

Sustainable local food economy

Business as a partner, not just a target for asks

Connecting local and corporate business into the food system through surplus, employment routes and genuine social value — not treating business engagement as an afterthought to the "real" work.

Fundraising, in common

Collective, not each organisation alone

A new, dedicated group testing what the partnership can raise together — on top of, not instead of, what individual members already do well on their own.

Who's in the room

An established partnership, deliberately restructuring in the open

Nobody is pretending the old structure worked perfectly. The point of doing this in front of the wider membership is so people can see, and shape, what replaces it.

Islington Council

Officers spanning poverty, public health and social care

Includes a councillor holding the Health & Social Care portfolio, a poverty-reduction lead, and the public health team — food named directly as a key part of that brief, not a side issue.

Community & sector partners

A refugee support forum, and community food organisations

Includes the director of a forum supporting people seeking safety, who also developed the borough's cost-of-living support programme — alongside community food organisations across the borough.

The Islington Food Partnership

An independent, council-funded body — not run inside the council

A small, part-time staff team coordinates the partnership itself, funded by the council but operating independently of it — a genuine structural difference from Camden's food partnership, which sits inside its council.

Coordinating the hub network

Felix Project convenes; Sustain is building the funding case

Felix Project has been working with at least ten boroughs already building or exploring a hub, coordinating the Hubs & Markets Working Group to share best practice and encourage collaboration instead of separate funding bids. That's led to Sustain building a consortium and sharing their National Lottery bid with boroughs, asking for expressions of interest.

The wider portfolio

Dark Matter Labs: the broader picture of London's buying power

Focused beyond hubs and civic infrastructure alone — on the collective buying power of London as a whole, including larger procurement and business purchases — and building a wider portfolio of work around that focus.

How this stays joined up

Coordination, not just conversation

A named risk from restructuring is that the four action groups drift apart. These are the mechanisms meant to stop that.

Now

The hub operator search, closing early October

Expressions of interest for the Regis Road hub operator are open, with site visits arranged — the concrete, physical piece of this work moving fastest right now.

Standing

Quarterly board meetings, with short written updates

Each action group now feeds the board a light-touch written highlight report before each quarterly meeting, rather than a long verbal update — proposed directly in the room as a way to respect people's time.

Cross-group habit

Shared agendas, and at least one person from every group at the board

Action groups share agendas with each other where their work overlaps, and each group has at least one representative sitting on the strategic board — so no group operates in a silo.

Standing

The London Hubs & Markets Working Group

Islington sits inside a wider, regularly-meeting group comparing real hub examples across boroughs — so lessons travel both ways, not just from Islington out.
