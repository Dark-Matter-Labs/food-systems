DRAFT — A PARTNER WORKSTREAM — HACKNEY FOOD HUB GROUP

Buying, storing and feeding Hackney, together.

An overview for everyone working on Hackney's food hub

A group of Hackney council officers, community food organisations and independent traders has been meeting to work out how the borough's food groups could buy, store and share food collectively, backed by shared infrastructure rather than each organisation fending for itself. This page is a single place to see what that's for, how it connects to work already happening across London, and how it stays coordinated as it develops.

WHY THIS EXISTS

Four things this has to do at once

This isn't only about finding a warehouse. It's about building something that does all four of these together — the same test the wider Food as Infrastructure portfolio holds every idea to.

01 — BUYING POWER

Collective, not each group alone

Hackney's food organisations currently buy separately, mostly at standard retail prices. Buying together — even at small scale — makes better food genuinely affordable, not just a values choice.

02 — A REAL CO-OP, NOT JUST A STORE

Owned and run by the groups that use it

The ambition is a collective food co-op: shared storage and buying power that Hackney's community organisations own and direct, not a service delivered to them from outside.

03 — RESILIENCE

Less exposed to the next shock

Shared storage and shared supplier relationships mean no single organisation's food access depends on one contract, one van, or one good year.

04 — BUILT TO LAST, NOT JUST TO COPE

Community capacity, not just emergency response

The point isn't only to move surplus food more efficiently. It's to build lasting organising and logistics capacity — a foundation to grow from over years, not a one-off pandemic-style response.

WHO'S IN THE ROOM

A small group, deliberately open to grow

Nobody is claiming to have this solved. The value of the group so far has been in comparing notes honestly — including admitting where it's stuck.

Hackney Council

Officers working on food and economic development

Looking at food hub infrastructure as part of the borough's wider economic development strategy — including how it could support small, independent local food shops, not only community groups.

Community & trade partners

Food co-ops, an independent grocer, and community food organisations

Includes an established local food cooperative and an independent grocer already trusted by community organisations across Hackney and neighbouring Haringey.

Coordinating the hub network

Felix Project convenes; Sustain is building the funding case

Felix Project has been working with at least ten boroughs already building or exploring a hub, coordinating the Hubs & Markets Working Group to share best practice and encourage collaboration instead of separate funding bids. That's led to Sustain building a consortium and sharing their National Lottery bid with boroughs, asking for expressions of interest.

The wider portfolio

Dark Matter Labs: the broader picture of London's buying power

Focused beyond hubs and civic infrastructure alone — on the collective buying power of London as a whole, including larger procurement and business purchases — and building a wider portfolio of work around that focus.

NOT STARTING FROM ZERO

How this connects to the wider work

Hackney is the newest borough working through this — but four others have already built, or are building, a version of the same thing. This effort sits inside a much larger, already-moving picture.

HOW THIS STAYS JOINED UP

Coordination, not just conversation

The group has been honest that it's felt directionless at times. These are the mechanisms meant to fix that.

Now

A structuring session, 22 September

A working session set specifically to bring clarity: who leads, what the first concrete step is, and how the small-scale pilot option gets tested — rather than another open-ended discussion.

Standing

The London Hubs & Markets Working Group, bi-monthly

Hackney now sits inside a wider, regularly-meeting group comparing real hub examples across boroughs — so lessons travel both ways, not just from Hackney out.
