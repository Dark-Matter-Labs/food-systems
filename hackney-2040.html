Draft. A working note from early conversations with Hackney Council about long-term foresight. It sets out how we understand the questions and how the work could run. It is not a council document, and nothing here is agreed policy.

Working note, October 2026
Dark Matter Labs, with Hackney, our home borough

What could Hackney be in 2040?

Hackney is starting to ask how the borough, and the council itself, can prepare for the next 13 years. Foresight can give leaders a shared language for that future and a way to test today’s plans against it. The vision stays Hackney’s own.

Themes from our first conversations

What we heard

These are the threads that came up most often. They are in our words and are a starting point for listening, not conclusions.

Prosperity

A strong economy that hasn’t brought poverty down

Hackney’s economy has grown, but many residents haven’t shared in it. Growth and fairness feel like they are on separate tracks.

Housing

Housing costs shape who can stay

The cost of a home is a main driver of hardship, and many people leave the borough as they get older. Who will still be able to live here in 2040 is an open question.

Direction

Many plans, looking for one north star

Strategies across the council need a common thread, so that each team can see how its work connects to a shared long-term direction.

Pressure

Rising need, tighter resources

Like every London borough, Hackney faces growing demand for services with less to meet it. Any long-term thinking has to help people prioritise, not add to their workload.

Change ahead

Climate, AI and work

Hotter summers and flooding, and AI reshaping the entry-level office and creative jobs Hackney has many of, will change what residents and businesses need.

Community

Social fabric, culture and partnership

Hackney’s strength is its communities and its voluntary sector. How the council works alongside them, and as one council, is part of the answer.

Policy coherence, not prediction

Why foresight, and what it is not

Across Europe, many councils find the biggest barrier to impact isn’t a lack of ambition. It is policy incoherence: long-term visions, spatial plans and sectoral strategies quietly pull in different directions.

Scenarios help by making a possible future concrete enough to react to. When leaders step into a plausible Hackney of 2040 together, the trade-offs between existing commitments become visible and discussable, rather than being found later in delivery.

  • A scenario isa plausible, tangible future condition that people can test their thinking against
  • It is nota forecast, a recommendation, or a vision written for Hackney
  • We bringtools, method and a setting for the conversation, plus lessons from other places
  • Hackney bringsits own knowledge, its people, and the decisions about what the future should hold
Foresight that reaches decisions

From today’s tensions to implications for existing plans

Foresight is only useful if it changes what happens next. This chain builds that link in from the start, so each scenario ends in a test of the plans already in motion.

A worked example: housing, affordability and growthIllustrative only
01Tension

Hackney needs more and better homes, but affordability already drives hardship and displacement. Growth could meet need, or deepen inequality.

02Question

How might growth be directed so that it narrows inequality between neighbourhoods and stays robust to economic and climate shocks?

03Scenario

It is 2040. Hackney has kept growing, but land and housing costs have risen sharply. More lower- and middle-income households struggle to stay, and climate pressures make some homes more expensive to adapt.

04Discussion

Who should still be able to live here? What would successful growth actually mean? What should growth protect, not just produce?

05Ingredient

Hackney remains a place where people on different incomes can afford to live, belong and build a life.

06Implication

Does the spatial plan support affordability and reduce displacement? Is the housing mix enough? How is public and community land used? Are homes being made resilient as well as low-carbon? Who benefits from growth, and who carries the costs?

A short, focused piece of work

How the work could run

Four steps over autumn 2026, ending in a handover. From there, Hackney’s own team carries what was learned into its long-term planning.

01, October

Listen

Read across existing plans and evidence for the tensions that matter most for 2040, and hold a small number of conversations with leaders across the council.

Out: a map of tensions and a shortlist of provocations
02, early November

Build scenarios

Three or four radical but plausible Hackney 2040 scenarios, each covering the borough and the council as an organisation.

Out: scenario cards and a short pre-read
03, November

Foresight session

A shared meal, then a half-day session where political and officer leaders work through the scenarios together.

Out: priorities, trade-offs and vision ingredients, in participants’ own words
04, December

Hand over

A short write-up of what the session revealed, with the scenarios and tools, so Hackney can keep using them.

Out: a findings note and a reusable toolkit
Then, led by Hackney

Hackney’s own plans

The council takes the findings into its leadership conversations and its long-term plan for the borough. The vision and the decisions are Hackney’s.

Why this matters beyond one plan

The wider context

The next decade will test every London borough. Planning for 2040 means planning for shocks as well as growth, and learning from places that have already started.

Climate

Heat, flooding and the cost of adapting homes and streets, alongside the transition to net zero.

Work and AI

Automation is likely to hit entry-level office and creative roles first. Those are pathways many young Hackney residents rely on.

Food

Supply shocks and rising prices reach households fastest through food. Local food infrastructure is part of a borough’s resilience.

Cost of living

Housing and living costs decide who can stay, and so who Hackney is for in 2040.

Lund, Sweden

Radical scenarios

We worked with Lund on radical scenarios that made climate targets concrete, for example by connecting carbon budgets to housing and construction choices.

What we learned: build in from the start how foresight feeds strategy, and how the organisation itself needs to change.

Newham, London

The Just Transition Plan

The UK’s first just transition plan combined climate action with social justice. It was adopted by Cabinet and launched at COP in December 2023.

365principlesfuturesenablers

What we learned: make the plan a tool that helps people do their jobs, and work out the internal shifts needed alongside the futures.

Across London

Food as Infrastructure

Boroughs including Camden, Islington and Hackney are working together on civic food infrastructure, sharing what they learn openly as they go.

Where it connects: food is one of the clearest places to see housing, health, the economy and climate meet.

Open, and Hackney’s to answer

Questions we are holding

None of these has a single right answer. The foresight work is a way of asking them together, with the people who will shape the answers.

  • Who should still be able to live in Hackney in 2040?
  • What should growth protect, not just produce?
  • What will the council need to become to serve the borough well?
  • Where do food, housing, work, health and climate meet, and where do they pull apart?
  • What would make the next 13 years feel like one journey rather than many plans?
