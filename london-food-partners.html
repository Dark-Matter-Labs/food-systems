Food as Infrastructure, London

Who we're building London's civic food system with

A working map of the boroughs, hubs and partners coming together around one idea: move the collective buying power of London behind a food system that's healthier, fairer and more resilient to shocks.

Where each borough stands

Camden, Islington and Hackney are active delivery partners. A further eight boroughs have meetings booked or under way, and seven more have been introduced, mostly through the Felix Project's own brokering. The map is schematic: each square is a borough, placed roughly where it sits in London.

Active partner Meeting booked Introduced, in discussion Not yet engaged

Kingston and Hounslow are introduced through their hub operators, and Tower Hamlets through a community food organisation, rather than the council directly.

An anchor already taking shape

Camden and Islington are exploring a shared food hub: up to £400,000 proposed over two years, around 15 tonnes of food a week if it proceeds, with collective buying built into the model from the start. It's still subject to an operator appointment process, so treat it as proposed rather than committed. More on the thinking behind it, and the wider working group coordinating hubs across boroughs, is on the Hubs & Markets Working Group page.

The partners working across London

Alongside the boroughs, a growing group of organisations are bringing hubs, supply, evidence and convening power to the work.

Hubs, redistribution and convening

  • The Felix Project — main connector, brokering borough and hub introductions
  • Sustain — aligning bids and shared working infrastructure
  • Greater London Authority — the London-wide policy route

Community food and local partnerships

  • Hackney Food Network — the borough's collective buying pilot group
  • Cooperation Town — Camden's community co-op network
  • Islington Food Partnership
  • Lewisham Local — food justice programme
  • Incredible Edible Lambeth
  • Borough hub operators across several further boroughs

Supply, retail and buying

  • Better Food Traders and Growing Communities — collective buying of UK-grown fruit and veg
  • Langridge Organic — organic wholesale supply
  • Wholegood, with the Young Camden Foundation — a delivered fruit pilot

Evidence, data and advice

  • TrackCarbon — supply-chain impact data
  • The Soil Association — production standards and procurement
  • Bremner & Co — food systems advisory
  • Real Zero and UCL — evidence on food, health and climate

How this grows

Most new introductions come through the Felix Project's own partnership team, and Sustain's London hubs network gives the work its wider convening. The London-wide portfolio sets out the shared framework all of this sits inside, and Options and Indicators for Camden shows what it looks like in practice in one borough.
