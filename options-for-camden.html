A short list, not the whole portfolio

Nine places where someone already showed up.

Camden's Food Partnership raised more than forty ideas across the Future Food Dinner and Workshop. Most are real and worth holding. Only a handful already have a named person or organisation ready to move. This page is those — picked for momentum, not importance, so Camden can start a real conversation with a real partner rather than another round of ideas.

The selection rule

Why these nine, and not the other thirty

Every idea on Where's the Energy? was tested against one question: did someone at the table already show up with real energy for it — a name, a business reason, a thing they're already doing — or is it still an idea waiting for an owner? The options below passed that test. Everything else is still live, just not yet ready for a partnership conversation; it stays on the full catalogue, not off the agenda.

The nine options

01

A Local Food Resilience Committee

Civic muscle

Add food formally to Camden's emergency response, alongside the police, hospital, ambulance and fire services already covered by the Borough Resilience Forum — before national policy requires it.

Energy already visible: Tanya brought the proposal and put her name to it directly.
What's needed next: who convenes it locally, and keeps the risk register alive after the first meeting.

02

A procurement charter

Demand

A joint public/private Camden charter set deliberately beyond existing certifications — Food for Life Served Here's gold standard only requires around 15% organic.

Energy already visible: Rosemary named it directly on the day — "if anyone wants to talk about a charter, let's do it."
What's needed next: who else wants in, and who owns the supplier relationships day to day.

03

Social value live-matchmaking

Hub

A real-time way to connect community projects with contractors and developers already on site, so in-kind support doesn't disappear once a contract ends.

Energy already visible: Kira, Camden's social value lead, is already doing versions of this.
What's needed next: whether she wants this turned into a repeatable process, or left as it is.

04

Camden food hub(s)

Hub

The single biggest tangible idea from the workshop — a hub-and-spoke model anchored at Regis Road, with composting and anaerobic digestion planned alongside it.

Energy already visible: Damien Conrad (Felix Project) is already directing the London Hubs & Markets Working Group carrying this forward.
What's needed next: who from Camden sits alongside him on it, day to day.

05

A collective buying engine

Hub

Scale an existing "one conversation instead of a hundred" relationship model into a repeatable, institutional capability rather than one person's own contacts.

Energy already visible: Carl's own business needs this to exist within six months — about as real as energy gets in the room.
What's needed next: who helps that urgency become an institutional capability, not just Carl's own relationships.

06

Sponsored or underwritten farmland

Supply

Camden-backed relationships with farms outside the borough, scaling proven cooperative wholesale models such as the Better Food Traders network rather than duplicating them.

Energy already visible: Dorothea pushed hardest for this at the table.
What's needed next: who follows up on the sponsorship-versus-ownership question she raised — still genuinely unresolved.

07

A Farmacy pilot

Hub

Pharmacies reframed as neighbourhood performance-and-nutrition nodes — fresh food, dietary advice and community links, framed around aspiration rather than illness.

Energy already visible: raised in the new-economics session; a business owner at the table (Carl) reacted to it live as an opportunity for his own pivot.
What's needed next: who scopes an actual pilot site and partner.

08

Deeper reach into non-traditional food actors

Civic muscle

Corner shops, convenience stores and small independent retailers sit outside most food-partnership conversations, despite being where many residents actually buy food.

Energy already visible: Farah is already doing some of this engagement.

A co-op toolkit and minimum standards

Hub

A recognisable, minimum-standards toolkit so anyone can start a food co-op, and residents can recognise one the way they'd recognise a supermarket or the Underground.

The one with a real-world deadline behind it

Cabinet Office consultation window closed 5 October 2026

Food still isn't formally part of Camden's emergency response. That window closing doesn't close the opportunity.

Tim Lang's National Preparedness Commission review recommended every local authority add a food resilience committee to its emergency planning; the Cabinet Office's call for views on making that statutory has now closed. Whichever way that lands nationally, a borough that has already set one up has done the work before being told to — and option 01 above already has a named person ready to carry it.

What to decide on purpose before picking a shortlist

None of the nine options above are free of a genuine trade-off. Naming them here is the point — these are Camden's decisions to make deliberately, not gaps this page is hiding.

Independence vs. council backing
How far any of these moves from council control, given even the most dynamic independent food partnerships nationally still rely on council funding and a seat at the table.
Sponsorship vs. ownership
Sponsoring farmland outside Camden (option 06) raises the same question as a council-sponsored co-op — how much control follows the money, and who decides.
Standards as inclusion vs. exclusion
A charter, minimum standards or conditions on traders (options 02 and 09) all raise the floor — but can lock out the smallest, least-resourced actors they're partly meant to help.
From options to a prioritised portfolio

What happens next

This page is deliberately a shortlist, not a plan. The next job — Camden's, not DML's alone — is testing each option's feasibility, deciding which ones Camden or a partner can run locally versus which need the wider alliance, and working out what resources and partners each one actually needs. That filtering is phase two's job; this page is what phase two filters.
