What Transfers to Camden

Camden asked for case studies and lessons from comparable work in Sweden and the Baltic States: what's transferable, and where Camden's context differs. This page is that answer. It's a tighter, Camden-facing companion to A Sovereign Food System — the fuller body of work Aleksander Nowak has been building on the ground in Sweden — read specifically against Camden's own institutions, powers and funding routes, rather than left as a general framework.

Why this comparison holds

Not a model to copy, a set of working instruments to test

Sweden's municipalities face the same underlying pressure Camden does: a food system priced around cheap calories and leakage, where the institutions footing the health, inequality and climate bill aren't the ones setting food prices. What's useful here isn't the Swedish policy environment — that's genuinely different, see below — it's that specific municipalities have already built and tested working instruments against that pressure, instrument by instrument, with real results attached. That's what's worth reading closely.

Read instrument by instrument, not as a package

Five things that transfer, directly or with adaptation

Each of these already has a real Swedish example behind it. The tag shows how directly it carries into Camden's own powers and partnerships.

Transfers directly Transfers with adaptation Doesn't transfer at Camden's scale
Transfers directlyPublic kitchens as a preventive-health lever

In Sweden: municipalities treat the food they already control — school meals, elderly care, public kitchens — as a preventive health tool rather than just a cost line. Sweden's own school-lunch reform has been linked to higher lifetime earnings, with the largest gains among children from poorer households.

For Camden: this needs no new legal mechanism. Camden already runs and commissions exactly these services. The instrument is simply the decision to specify and measure them against nutrition and preventive-health outcomes, not only cost and compliance.

Transfers directlyDe-risked transitional ownership for catering

In Sweden: for a service already outsourced to a for-profit contractor, a company majority-owned by the council from day one lets private capital cover the up-front cost of in-sourcing, repaid over time out of the contract itself. Ownership converts fully to the council once the private stake is repaid, without the council ever needing the full transition cost up front.

For Camden: this one is already live in Camden's own history, not a hypothetical. Camden looked at in-sourcing school catering outright and stepped back because the up-front cost was too high. This structure is the specific fix for that exact blocker, and is ready to revisit on that basis.

Transfers directlyHeadline indicators beyond £

In Sweden: a single public standard defines which foods qualify for public food programmes based on nutrition, sustainability and resilience, not brand or geography — and progress is tracked against that standard rather than spend alone.

For Camden: this is the same instinct behind Cornerstone Indicators ↗, already shared separately: a small, practical set of headline indicators that Camden's Food Mission can own and track directly, proportionate rather than an exhaustive measurement framework.

Transfers with adaptationFood as medicine, financed on avoided cost

In Sweden: regions carry the healthcare cost of diet-related disease directly, which makes them the natural financer of prevention. Targeted food prescriptions and dietary support for high-risk patients are financed upfront through health impact bonds, where investors fund the intervention and repayment is tied to the healthcare costs it verifiably avoids — Region Stockholm's diabetes-prevention bond is a live example.

For Camden: Camden isn't its own health authority the way a Swedish region is. The equivalent partner is North Central London ICB, a body Camden has to convene and partner with rather than instruct. The financing logic still transfers; it just needs that partnership built deliberately first, rather than assumed.

Doesn't transfer at Camden's scalePrice-shifting through tax and a local currency

In Sweden: municipalities can require part of a supplier payment in a local currency without breaching EU competition law, and the national VAT rate on food is a lever central government has already used as an entry point for shifting relative prices.

For Camden: neither instrument exists at borough level in England. VAT sits with central government, and English councils have no comparable legal path to issue their own currency. The underlying question — can Camden shift what food actually costs, even slightly — is still worth asking, just through different levers: procurement-linked local spend requirements, business rates relief, or making the national case, which is part of what The Overdue Bill is for.

Named plainly, not glossed over

Three structural differences worth naming

These aren't reasons the comparison fails. They're the reasons some instruments need a different route in, which is exactly what "transferable" has to mean if it's going to be useful rather than aspirational.

StructuralNo devolved tax or monetary levers

Sweden's VAT and e-krona tools don't exist for a London borough. UK fiscal and monetary policy sits with central government and the Bank of England, not Camden. Any instrument built on those levers has to be re-routed through national advocacy or through Camden's own procurement and commissioning powers instead.

StructuralA different health-system architecture

Sweden's regions directly run and fund healthcare, which is what lets them finance food-as-medicine instruments on their own authority. Camden's equivalent partner, North Central London ICB, is a separate body with its own governance. Every health-financed instrument above needs that partnership built first, not assumed as already in place.

StructuralNo Vinnova-equivalent at borough level

Swedish municipalities can draw on dedicated national innovation funding for exactly this kind of pilot. Camden's nearest equivalents — UKRI, the GLA, DHSC prevention funding, mission-aligned foundations — are more fragmented and have to be assembled deliberately. This is part of why DML's own co-funding commitment in Phase 1 matters: it's standing in for infrastructure Sweden's municipalities already have.

Said plainly, because it should be

What we're still building: the Baltic States

Camden's ask named the Baltic States alongside Sweden, and we want to be straightforward about where that stands rather than stretch a thin thread to match the Sweden material.

Early signalWhere this actually is right now

Aleksander Nowak spent time in Vilnius in October meeting people running food-security ventures across the Baltic States, and those conversations are real and ongoing. But they're conversations, not yet a developed case study with the kind of specific instrument, precedent and in-practice detail the Sweden material above has behind it.

Rather than pad this out to look more finished than it is, we're naming it honestly: this section needs real work before it can sit alongside the Sweden instruments on equal footing. We'll bring Camden a proper Baltic States case study as its own follow-up, with a date, rather than fold a thinner version into this close-out.

Where this leaves Phase 1

The third item on Ali's list

This page is the direct answer to one of the three things Ali and Ayanna named as the minimum to close Phase 1: case studies and lessons from Sweden and the Baltic States, with what's transferable and what differs made explicit rather than implied. Alongside Options for Camden and Cornerstone Indicators ↗, that's all three.

What's still open: the Baltic States case study, named above rather than quietly dropped. We'd rather close Phase 1 with one item honestly flagged than all three looking finished when one isn't.
