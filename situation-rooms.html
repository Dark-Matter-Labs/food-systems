A proposal from Dark Matter Labs, the Systems Transformation Hub and EIT Climate-KIC, November 2026

Situation rooms

We are proposing a series of small, private working sessions in which senior people from finance and policy work through a plausible near-term shock, follow it into their own institutions, and leave with decisions they can act on. The first is about food.

Summary

Most of the people who run Europe’s large financial and public institutions now accept that climate and nature risk is systemic. Far fewer have changed how capital is allocated as a result. In our experience the gap is rarely one of awareness. The risks arrive together, through food, energy, water and labour, while the models institutions rely on still look at them one at a time.

Situation rooms are our response. Each brings eight to ten decision-makers together around one carefully prepared scenario and the data needed to explore it. We are starting with food, because that is where connected risks become visible fastest. We are seeking €250,000 to begin, as the first step towards a programme of around €1 million a year.

The problem

A poor harvest does not stay a food problem for long. When fertiliser and energy costs are already high and several growing regions are hit in the same season, the effects move quickly into the financial system. Food prices rise and stay high, because people cannot simply buy less food. Central banks face inflation that higher interest rates do little to cure. Insurers see claims rise and begin to withdraw cover. Lenders to farms and food businesses watch their loan books weaken, investors reprice, and governments find themselves paying for emergency support just as inflation squeezes their budgets.

This is not a distant prospect. The Systems Transformation Hub’s risk outlook for 2026 and 2027 describes pressures that could play out within a single reporting cycle. Yet the cost of keeping people fed through a disruption of this kind sits on no institution’s balance sheet. At present it is paid for in the middle of a crisis, when it is most expensive.

The appetite to address this is growing. Since the autumn, leaders of financial institutions, including several UK pension funds, have approached us directly to ask how they might start. What they tend to describe is the absence of a place to work the problem through: somewhere they can look at the risk honestly, with the people they would need to act alongside, without the conversation becoming public.

What we propose

A situation room is a facilitated, off-the-record session built around one plausible shock. Most of the work happens before anyone enters the room. We speak with every participant individually beforehand to understand what they are exposed to, what they are able to change and where their hands are tied. We build the scenario, the data and the visualisation around what we hear, and we rehearse the session in full.

On the day, participants follow the shock from its origin into their own institutions. The central question is one an insurer might ask about a house: if this risk is plausible, how much of your book should be going towards managing it, and through what? Participants do not need to share our view of the risk, and we encourage them to bring their own models to test against the scenario.

Each room looks at two horizons: what could be done now to make the shock less damaging, and what needs to be built so that the next one does less harm. The most useful investments often serve both. In the London Borough of Camden, for example, the council is investing in a local food hub that serves everyday supply and will also give the borough something to rely on when supply is disrupted.

We hold to one principle throughout. We invite the people who hold the relevant decisions, so that the discussion cannot end with responsibility being passed to someone who is not present. Each participant should leave with a decision within their own remit that they intend to test over the following six months, an account of the risk they can take back to colleagues and supervisors, and working relationships with others in the room.

The first room

The first room concerns food and the financial system. Its scenario begins in spring, after a winter of high energy and fertiliser prices, as farmers cut back on nitrogen and a strong El Niño affects several major growing regions at once. It follows the resulting price shock through the summer and into the institutions represented in the room. It draws on Dark Matter Labs’ work on nutritional continuity, including a model of how a single food shock cascades into social and financial risk, and on the Systems Transformation Hub’s analysis of how disruption spreads across Europe.

We expect the room to include central banks and supervisors, public and development banks, insurers and reinsurers, banks that lend to farming and food, pension funds and other long-term investors, and the policymakers and regional leaders who decide what happens on the ground. We are looking for people at director level or above who decide how capital is allocated, or who set the rules that govern it.

After food

Food is the pilot. The same approach can be applied to other connected risks, and we expect to turn next to labour, and then to heat and water. Each room should add to a shared understanding of how a failure in one system is passed on to others. Behind the rooms we intend to build a small standing capability: a team, a way of bringing in modelling from participating institutions, and the visualisation that lets a room see a cascade as it unfolds.

What we hope will change

We are working towards three outcomes that depend on one another. In finance, we want institutions to understand the cascading risks on their books and act on them, and we want fresh thinking on how central banks and monetary policy respond before public finances run out of room. In policy, we want risks of this kind to be planned for in advance rather than improvised in a crisis. And in a small number of regions willing to move first, we want money to reach the infrastructure that keeps people fed and supplied, with public banks and other partners alongside.

We will not pretend to know exactly where each room will lead. Once a group has genuinely grasped a risk, the next steps tend to become clearer, and we would rather follow that than promise a fixed answer at the outset.

Cost and support

We estimate the full programme at around €1 million a year. We are seeking €250,000 in seed funding to begin: to put the team in place, prepare and run the first food rooms, and build the data and modelling they depend on. Over time we expect financial institutions to fund rooms that serve them directly.

There are several ways to support the work. Funders can contribute to the core capability or to a room on a particular subject. Institutions can host a room, share modelling or data from their own risk work, or introduce us to the people who ought to be in the room. Our aim is to have seed funding in place and a date and venue for the first room confirmed by the end of 2026, with the room itself early in 2027.

Who we are

The programme is a partnership of three organisations. The Systems Transformation Hub brings systems approaches to European policymaking; it was founded by the Club of Rome, the World Resources Institute, EIT Climate-KIC, Metabolic and Systemiq. EIT Climate-KIC is a climate innovation agency supported by the European Institute of Innovation and Technology. Dark Matter Labs works on the institutional and financial infrastructure needed for the transition, including Food as Infrastructure, its work on London’s food system.
