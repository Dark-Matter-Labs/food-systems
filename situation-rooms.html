Situation rooms for the risks finance can’t yet see

Senior people in finance and policy already accept that climate and nature risk is systemic. That knowledge has not yet changed where capital goes. We run small, off-the-record situation rooms that trace one near-term shock to the decisions the people in the room actually hold. Food is the pilot for a programme that will cover other interconnected risks.

The risk is coupled, and most models aren’t

Food, energy, water and labour risks now feed back on one another. A thin harvest or a blocked chokepoint shows up within months as food inflation that interest rates can’t fix, as insurance claims and withdrawn cover, as weaker loan books, as repricing across portfolios and as calls on public budgets already near their limits. These are not 2050 scenarios. They sit inside one reporting cycle.

Most institutions still model each risk on its own. Where they try to model how risks cascade, they rarely do it well, and the cost of keeping people fed when systems break sits on no one’s balance sheet. So it gets paid for in the crisis instead of priced in advance.

What a situation room is

A small group of people who hold real decisions, in a space well supported by data, having conversations they can’t have in public. Together they work through one shock and the options for responding to it: in policy, in finance, and in the places where the risk actually lands.

Where it starts

Participants know the risk is systemic. What they lack is a clear line from a near-term shock to their own book, and the words to make that case to their boards, supervisors and investors.

Where it ends

Each participant leaves with one decision within their own remit to test in the next six months, a plain case to take back to colleagues, and named follow-ups with others in the room.

The rule

No action leaves the room assigned to someone who isn’t in it. We invite the people with the remit to act, so “that’s for the regulator” is never the answer.

Two horizons in every room

What can be done now to make the shock less damaging, and what has to be built so the next one hurts less. The most useful moves often do both, like a city food hub that is built for everyday supply and also holds the line when supply breaks.

The first room: how a food shock reaches the balance sheet

We start with food because it is where coupled risk turns concrete fastest, and because people can’t buy less of it. The scenario builds on Dark Matter Labs’ Nutrition Continuity work and the Systems Transformation Hub’s near-term risk outlook.

  1. The growing season. After a winter of high energy and fertiliser prices, farmers cut nitrogen use, and a strong El Niño stresses several breadbaskets at once.
  2. The price shock. Import-dependent food prices rise, and food inflation stays high after energy prices ease.
  3. Into the financial system. Central banks face tightening into a supply shock. Insurers meet claims and pull back cover. Food and farming loan books weaken. Portfolios and import-dependent sovereign bonds reprice. Public budgets absorb emergency support just as inflation squeezes them.

Who is in the room

Eight to ten people at director level or above who direct capital flows or set the rules: the people who decide allocation, lending, underwriting or supervision, alongside the policymakers and regional leaders who decide what happens on the ground.

A series, not a one-off

Food is the pilot. The same method then moves to the next pressure points: labour, then heat and water. Each room reuses the format with a new shock and the institutions most exposed to it, and each adds to a shared picture of how one system’s failure feeds the next.

Behind the rooms sits a standing capability: a small team, a way of bringing in modelling from participating institutions, and clear data visualisation, so each new room starts from what the last one learned.

What it is for

Three outcomes that reinforce one another. We aim to deliver all three.

Finance that prices continuity

Institutions that understand the cascading risk on their books and act on it, and new thinking at the level of central banks and monetary policy.

Policy that prepares

Risk built into policy before the crisis, so governments don’t improvise and load up liabilities when it arrives.

Regions that move

Front-runner regions where resources actually move into the infrastructure that keeps people fed and supplied, with public banks and other partners alongside.

Why now

  • The timing is near-term. The pressures on food, energy and climate are converging now, not in a distant scenario.
  • Demand is coming to us. Leaders of financial institutions, including UK pension funds, have started approaching us directly to ask how to do this work for themselves.
  • The gap is clear. Risk teams are trying to model cascading risk and finding it hard. A trusted, off-the-record space to work through it does not yet exist.

How we run it

  • Every participant first. We speak to each participant one to one before the room, to understand their exposure, what they could change and where their hands are tied.
  • Led by people who know the field. Each room has a lead who works on that topic day to day, alongside people who have worked inside the industries we convene and speak their language.
  • Rehearsed and data-led. We run each session in full beforehand, and build the data and visualisation that let a room see the cascade.
  • Plain language. Finance terms, near-term horizons, no jargon.
  • A clear structure. Senior leads from the partner organisations set direction. A dedicated delivery team runs the work.

Timeline

  1. By the end of 2026. Seed funding in place, and the first room’s date and venue set.
  2. Early 2027. One-to-one conversations with every participant, a full rehearsal, then the first food room.
  3. Through 2027. A second food room, then the next topics: labour, then heat and water.
